A civic rights movement

A city should help you live. Not just exist.

Every person deserves a safe, healthy, accessible, sustainable and accountable civic environment — and government should have a measurable duty to provide it.

01 — Fundamental civic rights

11 things every city should get right.

These are proposed civic entitlements: practical conditions that make dignified urban life possible.

01

Safe Mobility

Roads, crossings, signals and transport that do not make everyday movement unnecessarily dangerous.

02

Clean Air

Protection from preventable air pollution and transparent monitoring of air quality.

03

Safe Water

Reliable, safe drinking water and rapid action when contamination occurs.

04

Safe Habitat

Buildings, drainage and neighbourhood infrastructure designed and maintained for safety.

05

Clean Surroundings

Scientific waste management, sanitation and clean public spaces.

06

Nature & Public Space

Access to parks, trees, rivers, hills and public spaces protected for future generations.

07

Accessibility

Streets, transport and public services usable by children, seniors and people with disabilities.

08

Emergency Response

Fast, coordinated civic response to flooding, fires, road hazards and other emergencies.

09

Peace & Reasonable Noise

A reasonable right to sleep, study, work and live without preventable disturbance.

10

Functional Governance

Public services that are reachable, trackable and designed around citizens rather than departments.

11

Accountability

Clear responsibility, measurable service standards and consequences when duties are repeatedly ignored.

02 — The core mechanism

Right → Duty → Standard → Accountability.

A right becomes meaningful when it creates a duty, the duty has a measurable standard, and failure has a transparent path to correction.

Right

Define the civic condition every person should be able to expect.

Duty

Assign a responsible public authority with a duty of care.

Standard

Publish measurable service levels and minimum safety standards.

Accountability

Escalate unresolved failures and publish performance.

03 — Measure the city

The Right to Live Index.

Cities should be judged by the conditions people experience — not only by projects announced or money spent.

Possible measures include safe mobility, walkability, air quality, water and sanitation, public transport, public-space access, civic response times and accountability.
