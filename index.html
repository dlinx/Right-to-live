A citizen movement for better everyday life

We don't want a perfect city.
We want a city that works.

We pay taxes. We follow rules. We build our lives here. Broken roads, unsafe streets, dirty air, flooding and endless complaints should never become “normal”.

This isn't about blaming a city.
It's about refusing to accept bad civic life as normal.
01 — The everyday problem

You shouldn't need a VIP pass to live with dignity.

A city works when an ordinary person can walk home safely, breathe reasonably clean air, get safe water, use public spaces and get a broken civic service fixed.

“Just be careful.”Why is that the solution?
“Everyone faces it.”That doesn't make it okay.
02 — What a city owes you

11 basics. Not luxury. Not VIP treatment.

These are the minimum conditions for a safe, healthy and dignified life.

03 — This is a movement

Less complaining.
More demanding. More measuring. More doing.

We don't need another angry WhatsApp group. We need citizens who turn frustration into evidence, evidence into demands, and demands into measurable change.

01 · See it

Spot a civic problem in your street, neighbourhood or city.

02 · Document it

Photos, location, dates, data. Turn frustration into evidence.

03 · Demand it

Ask for a responsible authority, a deadline and a measurable outcome.

04 · Track it

Follow up. Publish the result. Support what works. Challenge what doesn't.

04 — The idea

Turn “please fix this” into a civic right.

Every right should create a government duty. Every duty should have a measurable standard. Repeated failure should have a visible path to accountability.

RIGHT

What people should be able to expect.

DUTY

Who in government owns the responsibility.

STANDARD

The time and quality target.

ACCOUNTABILITY

What happens when the duty is repeatedly missed.

05 — Measure what matters

If we can't measure it, we can't improve it.

The Right to Live Index can score what people actually experience: safe mobility, walkability, air, water, public transport, green space, response times and accountability.

06 — Your city. Your voice.

Don't just say “someone should do something.”
Be the someone.

Start with one street. One problem. One piece of evidence. Then find ten people who care. Movements don't begin with millions. They begin when ordinary people decide that “good enough” isn't good enough anymore.
